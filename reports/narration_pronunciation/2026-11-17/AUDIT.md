# French mathematical letters and repeated vowel wording

## Scope and result

The 73 catalogue lesson sources were reviewed for isolated mathematical letters
and adjacent repeated vowel sounds. The shared teaching narration policy is now
centralized in [`tools/tts.py`](../../../tools/tts.py) and applied by
[`TeachingAzureService`](../../../tools/teaching_voiceover.py).

32 scene scripts and six shared exercise data files needed explicit narration
edits. Displayed formulas, explicit captions, navigation, authored timing values
and bookmark names/order were preserved. The shared pronunciation changes also
apply to existing letter helpers in otherwise unchanged lessons.

## Implemented behavior

- One French spoken-name mapping covers the Latin alphabet. Written letters and
  case remain in captions; the MAI request receives names such as « iks » and
  « i grec ». Existing character-spelling directives use the same mapping.
- Mathematical lowercase letters `a` and `y` are explicitly marked in the
  reviewed narration. Ordinary French uses, elisions and hyphenated words remain.
  Other unambiguous isolated letters are normalized at the MAI synthesis boundary.
- Adjacent identical vowel words involving a mathematical letter are rejected
  before cache lookup, including across bookmarks. Rephrasing resolves the
  demonstrated collisions. Deliberate expressions such as « a fois a » remain.
  Positive authored breaks and punctuation can separate intentional repetitions;
  a zero-duration break cannot bypass the check.
- Pronunciation directives cannot contain bookmarks that would split one symbol
  between separate synthesis requests. Tests verify original bookmark coordinates,
  fragment order, exact PCM sample counts and authored silence.
- Prepared synthesis inputs identify corrected cache entries. Unchanged inputs
  can reuse existing caches; no old recordings are deleted. The native-voice
  boundary-event path and the current teaching voice/rate are retained.

Vowel wording was revised in lessons 01, 23, 26, 27, 33, 36, 37 and 59. Lesson 23
now uses « Les coordonnées du point A sont… » and the matching construction for
point B. Lesson 27 uses « l'inverse de A » consistently.

Changed scene numbers: 01, 02, 06, 07, 11, 12, 14, 15, 16, 23, 24, 25, 26, 27,
28, 29, 30, 31, 33, 36, 37, 39, 43, 51, 52, 57, 59, 60, 64, 67, 71 and 73.
The data changes are `practice_questions.yaml` and worked examples for
`elimination_variables`, `equations_droite_plan`, `lois_sinus_cosinus`,
`programmation_lineaire` and `regle_cramer`.

## Audio status and remaining validation

No Azure speech was synthesized, no lesson was rendered, and no existing media
export was replaced in this iteration. Azure credentials are unavailable in the
editing environment. The sample-accurate audio tests use local synthetic fixtures;
they are not listening evidence.

The source validator addresses repeated wording and invalid pronunciation splits.
It does not detect or guarantee a cure for a vowel stutter generated by the speech
model. Azure documents [pronunciation aliases and character spelling](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-synthesis-markup-pronunciation)
and [text-only pronunciation elements](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/speech-synthesis-markup-structure).
The [MAI documentation](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/ai-services/speech-service/mai-voices)
does not specify a deterministic repeated-vowel remedy.
